Butler Guide

Tasks, reminders, notes and a calendar in one place, with reminders on your phone through Microsoft To Do. Everything here is what's in the app today.

Every name and figure in this guide is made up.

What Butler is

Butler keeps your tasks, notes and events in one data file in OneDrive, saved a couple of seconds after every change. You can edit on the laptop and on the phone; if both changed things, Butler puts them together. Microsoft To Do also puts your tasks on the phone with their reminders.

Butler is the app's new name (it was "PA App"). Only the name you see changed: the file is still PA App.html, so your links and installed app keep working, and the OneDrive folder and the To Do list are still called "PA App".

My DayToday's tasks, what's overdue, and today's events.
TasksEvery task, on a board or in a list.
CalendarThe month, with tasks, events and coming repeats.
NotesFree-form notes, saved as you type.
NotificationsEvery message from the last 30 days.
SettingsThe data file, backups, phone reminders, buckets and labels.

Install it as an app

Open Butler's web address in Chrome, then install it so it opens in its own window like any other program:

  1. Open Butler in Chrome.
  2. Click ⋮ (top right) › Cast, save, and share › Install page as app.
  3. From then on, open it from the Start menu or the taskbar.

On the phone, open the same address in Chrome, then ⋮ › Add to Home screen (or Install app) › Install. Butler then opens from its own icon, full screen.

The Android app (being built) is Butler too, inside an app of its own, and will add what a web page can't: reminders that ring from the phone itself, sharing photos and bills to Butler, and home-screen widgets. To install it, open the repo's Releases page on the phone, download Butler.apk and open it; the first time, Android asks to allow installing apps from Chrome. A newer one installs over it and keeps your sign-in. Sign in to OneDrive works the same, except Microsoft's page opens inside the app and comes back by itself. Butler updates itself as usual: a new APK is only needed when the app around it changes.

Reminders from the app. In the app, Settings › Ring reminders from chooses who rings your reminders on that phone: To Do (as before) or Butler app. On Butler app, Android rings each coming reminder on a task you're on, even with Butler closed, and pressing one opens its task; the first time, Android asks to allow notifications. It's per phone, rings with Butler's own chime and shows a bell at the top of the screen. About every 15 minutes, even with Butler closed, the app also checks the data file, so a reminder added on the laptop rings on the phone too (if the check is held back, it rings once the app has opened). A reminder changed or deleted elsewhere in the last 20 minutes or so before it's due may still ring once at the old time. To Do still rings too until you turn off its notifications on that phone. Take photo opens the camera in the app as it does in the phone's browser.

Share › Butler. In another app (the gallery, WhatsApp, a PDF, a web link), press Share and pick Butler. A sheet asks where it goes: a New task with the files attached (named after the first file; shared words go in its notes), a New note (words only), or Add to… anything you already have. Add to… lists every place that can take it, soonest tasks and newest bills first: tasks, assets, connections, bills (as the bill's scan), Vault documents and folders, upkeep entries, agreements, notes (words only) and musings. It shows what the person picker shows, so with everyone shown it lists someone else's tasks too (a bill task of theirs, say), with their name. Type in the search box (a car, a bill, a folder…) or tap a chip (Tasks, Assets, Bills…) to narrow it down, pick one and press Add; it then opens. Words or a link go below its notes; a Vault folder keeps only the files. The phone must be signed in to OneDrive to save; if Butler was closed, it waits a moment to read the latest first.

Morning and evening. At 8:00 am the phone shows what's on today, as My Day has it (late ones, timed ones with their times, any-time ones and events); at 8:00 pm, what's still to do today (nothing if it's all done). Tap it to open My Day. It comes even when Butler is closed (Butler brings the words up to date a few minutes before), and needs the Butler app version 7. Change the times or turn it off in Settings › Morning and evening (each device its own). On the laptop it pops up while Butler is open; if Butler wasn't open then, it's waiting in Notifications next time.

Widgets. Hold an empty spot on the home screen › Widgets › Butler, and drag one out. My Day shows what the My Day page shows: Late, Today, Any time today and Coming up (the next 7 days). One list shows the open tasks in one bucket, soonest due first; it asks which bucket when you add it, and holding it lets you pick another. Pressing a task opens it in Butler; pressing the name at the top opens My Day (or Tasks). They're brought up to date whenever Butler on the phone opens, reads the latest or is put away, and also about every 15 minutes with Butler closed. Press ↻ on a widget to bring both up to date now: it says "Bringing this up to date…" for a moment, without opening Butler. Either way it needs the phone to be signed in to OneDrive and online; if it's still out of date, the widget says so.

Back. In the app, the phone's back gesture (or back button) does what Esc does on the laptop: it closes the photo, menu, question, pop-up or task that's open, one at a time, and steps back from Vehicles or Property to Assets. With nothing open, it leaves Butler, which stays in the background and opens again at once.

Signing in in the app opens Microsoft's page in a sheet over Butler; pick your account and it comes back by itself. It stays signed in for weeks, not just a day. If reminders added elsewhere seem late, let Butler run in the background: phone Settings › Apps › Butler › Battery › Unrestricted.

Opens without internet. Once you've opened Butler online, it keeps a copy of itself, so the installed app opens even offline; each time it's online it picks up the latest version. On the laptop you can keep working offline with your data file. On the phone, without a connection, Butler shows your last copy to read, and editing comes back once you're online. Your data never leaves your devices and OneDrive, apart from the copy of your tasks sent to To Do.

The first time

  1. Settings › Create new data file, or Open existing data file if you have one. Put it in a synced folder such as OneDrive so the phone can see it.
  2. Settings › Choose backup folder: one dated copy a day, automatically.
  3. Open any task › Choose attachments folder for files you attach.
  4. Settings › Phone reminders › Connect Microsoft To Do, to get reminders on your phone (see chapter 5).
The sidebar says where you stand. At the bottom left, "Saved to file" means you're safe. "Browser only" means no data file is linked yet.

My Day

The first screen is your day as one timeline. The line under the title says how many tasks and events there are today, and how many are late from before.

  • Late: tasks from earlier days that aren't done, pinned on top.
  • Today: today's tasks with a time and today's events, in time order. A teal line marks now; a task whose time has passed shows its time in red.
  • Any time today: tasks due today with no time, all-day events, and anything you added to My Day.
  • Done today: what you ticked off today, newest first.
  • Beside it (below it on the phone): Coming up for the next 7 days, and Anytime for tasks with no date.

Search

The Search box at the top of the sidebar (on the phone, the magnifying glass above the page) looks through everything: tasks (and their notes and steps), notes, events, assets, documents (by name, type or number), zakat records and payments. Type a few words; each one must be there, in any order. Results are grouped by kind; click one to open it. With the person picker on one person, it searches only theirs.

Filter this page: Tasks, Assets, Utilities, Zakat, the Vault, Notifications and the Bin each have their own box, which narrows that page's list as you type.

Button colours: gold buttons do something (Transfer, Select, Paste history, Export); teal ones add an entry, something that happens on a day (+ Task, + Rates, + Zakat record); outlined ones add to a list (+ Asset, + Connection, + Document); red ones delete. On a page they go gold, then teal, then outlined, left to right, and the add buttons say what they add.

The ⋮ menu at the end of every page's buttons opens a panel down the right edge with every page's buttons in one place: Actions (the gold ones), Add an entry (teal) and Add to a list (outlined), each with the page it's on, then Data, Export, the Guide, Light or dark, Settings and the Bin. Pick one and Butler goes to that page and presses it. Esc, × or a tap outside closes it.

Every page is laid out the same: its name with its buttons on the right and ⋮ at the end (on a phone, the first teal button and ⋮), then the tabs with Filter this page beside them, then the list's Group by and Sort, with how many and their total on the right.

Photos

Pictures show a small preview wherever files are listed (a task's files, a document's or asset's, the Folders tab). Click one and it opens large inside Butler; Open the task (or asset, entry, document) goes to what it's on. The arrows (or a swipe on the phone) go through that item's photos; Esc, × or a click outside closes it. Open shows the file itself in a new tab, and Save keeps a copy in your Downloads. Other files, such as PDFs, open in a new tab as before.

Musings

Musings (sidebar; on a phone, More › Musings) keeps pieces you've written: poems, stories, letters, thoughts.

  • + Piece opens it to write: a title, the date written, draft or finished, and collections (commas between, for example Poems, Travel). It saves as you type.
  • Formatting, typed or with the buttons: a line starting # is a heading (## a smaller one), > a quote, - a list; **bold** and *italics*; a blank line starts a new paragraph. The word count follows as you go.
  • Pictures: a cover, shown whole above the title, and a gallery at the end with a caption for each. They're kept in the attachments folder (on the phone, OneDrive), named after the piece ("Rain on the roof 2026.jpg"; "Untitled piece" before it has a title), and renamed to match when you change the title and press Done. Tapping one opens it large.
  • Done shows the piece to read, like a page. Print or save as PDF prints just the piece.
  • The list shows each piece with its cover small beside it; Drafts and Finished narrow it, and it groups by collection, status or year. Search finds pieces by their words.

People

Butler can keep things for your family too. Add each person in Settings › People, for example "Sam". Everything you already had is yours.

  • Whose things to show: once there's someone besides you, a box at the top of the sidebar (on a phone, above the page) picks Everyone or one person. My Day, Tasks, the Calendar, Notes and Assets then show only that person's things. Each device remembers its own choice.
  • New things go to the person being shown (to you when showing Everyone).
  • Several people: a task's panel has a People row, and a note shows its people above it. + Add puts someone on it, × takes them off. Something always belongs to at least one person.
  • Names beside a task, note or document show, in full and in each person's colour, who it's for when it isn't just the person being shown. Each person gets the next free colour when added; change it in Settings › People. You have none, so your own things stay plain.
  • Reminders and To Do: only tasks you're on remind you and go to your To Do. A task that's only Sam's stays in Butler; if you want reminding, add yourself to it. Taking yourself off a task removes it from your To Do.
  • Zakat day: each person can have one, as an Islamic date (for example 15th Sha'ban).
  • Removing someone asks first. Anything that was only theirs becomes yours, their share of an asset too.

Assets

Assets in the sidebar keeps what you own and owe, and what it's worth on any day.

  • Tabs: Overview, then one for each kind you have (Gold, Silver, Cash and accounts, Property, Vehicles, Investments, Owed to you, Debts, Other), then Rates. On a phone the tabs scroll sideways.
  • + Asset: pick the kind and a name. Gold asks its karat and weight (grams or tola; 1 tola is 11.7 g), silver its weight. Everything else asks its currency and amount on a day: a balance, a value, money owed to you, or what's still owed on a debt.
  • Rows show the details: gold and silver's weight in the unit you typed it in (the other in brackets), the date you've had it since, its first photo, notes, and its owners by name with their shares.
  • Zakatable: each asset has this tick. Savings, gold, silver, investments and money owed start ticked; property, vehicles and other things start unticked. Change it for anything that's different, and zakat records start from it.
  • Amounts by date: open an asset and type a new amount to keep it beside the old ones, or use Update balances to type every account's balance for one day. A value on a day is the latest amount on or before it. The asset's Value history (Balance history for an account, Still owed, by date for a debt) lists them; change a date or amount right there, or remove one.
  • Amounts are grouped the local way as you type (9,00,000), everywhere in Butler.
  • Automatic rates: once a day, a day with no rates gets the world prices (gold and silver, and the dollar and any other currency you use), marked Automatic, so the charts have something for the days in between. They're world prices, not the local sarafa rate: type the sarafa rate on the days that matter (above all your zakat day), and it replaces the fill-in. A zakat record warns if its day only has a fill-in. Only prices are asked for; nothing about you is sent. Turn it off on the Rates tab.
  • Rates: gold (each karat) and silver in rupees per tola, and other currencies in rupees, by day. Gold, silver and foreign accounts are valued with the latest rates on or before the day. Something with no rate yet says so instead of counting as nothing. Each day of rates is named by its Islamic date, with the normal date in brackets; click a day to change its rates or its date, or to delete it.
  • The buttons (Transfer, + Rates, Update balances, + Asset) are on every tab.
  • Owners: with people added, an asset can be shared, for example 50% each. Transfer gives it to someone else from a date on; who had it before is kept, so earlier days still count them.
  • Debts are taken off your net worth. A sold car or a closed account gets a "sold or closed on" date, so it stops counting from then but its history stays.
  • Overview: net worth and cards for gold, silver, cash and debts, a line of your net worth at the end of each month for the last year (point at a dot for its value, or open it as a table), and bars by kind.
  • Whose: the person picker shows one person's assets and their share of each, or everyone's.

Rented-out property

Assets › Property opens the Property page (‹ Assets at the top goes back). Its Properties tab lists them (each one rented shows Rented out); its Rentals tab keeps your rental agreements. At the top of Rentals: the rent due this month, what's come in this year, and what's owed.

  • + Agreement (or Rent it out in a property's pop-up): a name, the properties it covers (one or several), the tenant and phone, the day rent is due, the deposit, and the first term: when it starts, for how many months (11 to start with) and the rent. It makes a monthly rent task, "Plaza lease Rent (Nov 26)" (the month moves on with each one; no reminder), and a Renew task a month (or as chosen) before the term ends.
  • The ledger, in the agreement, is your rent sheet: every month from the start, with what was due, what came in, when, the balance and a note ("Start of agreement", "Renewed, 10% increase", a write-off). The total owed is at the top.
  • Record rent (or ticking Collect rent) asks how much came in and when, suggesting what was paid last time. Paying less leaves the rest owed. Write off (unticked unless you tick it) clears what's still owed for that month. Tap a month in the ledger to add to it or remove a payment.
  • Renew (or ticking the Renew task) starts the next term: it suggests 10% more, unrounded; type the rent the new agreement says. The month it starts is marked with the increase.
  • Terms: the agreement lists each term (each renewal), newest first, with its dates, rent and increase. Tap one to change its start, length, rent or note, or delete it (if it was a mistake; the first can't be). Add its agreement there keeps that term's signed agreement in the agreement's folder, named after the term ("Plaza lease Jul 2025 – Jun 2026.pdf").
  • Tasks: the agreement shows its Collect rent and Renew tasks with their next dates (tap one to open it). If Collect rent was deleted, Make it brings it back. Changing Rent due on day moves the next Collect rent to that day.
  • Paste history brings in a rent sheet you kept in Excel: select its rows (Month, Due, Received, Payment date, Balance, Comments), copy, and paste. Before you bring it in, it says how many months it found, the rent changes, and what was due, received and short, so you can check it against the sheet's Total; lines it doesn't understand are named and left out. Each month the rent changed becomes a new term (and is marked "Renewed, x% increase"), what came in each month becomes a payment with its date, and your own comments are kept. Pasting again replaces those months, so nothing doubles.
  • Files (the agreement, receipts) go to Rentals › <name> in your folder, from Add files on the agreement or attached to its tasks.
  • Several properties in one agreement: each property's pop-up shows the agreement's rent "for both" (or for all of them) and names the others. To split it, give each property its share (%) in the agreement's Details, adding up to 100: each property then shows its share of the rent and of what's owed. The ledger stays the agreement's.
  • Ended on, when the tenant leaves, stops the ledger and the renew reminders. Deleting an agreement sends it and its open tasks to the Bin.

Upkeep: cars and other assets

Open any asset and its Upkeep keeps what it costs to look after: a car's oil changes and repairs, a house's AC service.

  • A car (a vehicle asset) also keeps its make, model, years, number plate and the km when bought. Leave the name empty and it's named from make, model and plate.
  • + Upkeep: date, type (Service, Repair, Added for things bought for it, Other), item, cost, vendor, notes and, for a car, the reading. Item and vendor suggest what you've typed before. Once saved, add a photo or the bill.
  • What it's cost: this year and all time, by type and by item, each year's total, per km, and with the price you paid. The Assets overview says what upkeep cost this year.
  • Readings: every entry with a reading counts, and Reading adds one between services, so Butler knows how far the car goes in a month.
  • The plan: repeating jobs, every so many km and/or months, whichever comes first. A car starts with engine oil, air filter, AC filter, coolant, brake oil and a battery check; change, add or take off any. Each job has a task for the asset's owners, due when the months run out or, by km, on the day the average distance says it will get there (it moves as readings come in).
  • Vehicles page: cars have their own page, under Assets: Assets › Vehicles opens it, and ‹ Assets at the top goes back. Pick all vehicles or one at the top. Overview has a card per car (reading, upkeep this year and all time, what's overdue and next) and upkeep by year; Log every entry; Plan every job with its due date (red when late, amber within 30 days); Tasks the open upkeep tasks; Store your car supplies; Details one car's details, and the button to change them. A car opened from Assets opens here.
  • Store: + Store supplies keeps supplies bought for the cars (how many, cost, vendor, a photo of the bill). Use on a car moves one or more into that car's log at their share of the cost; the rest stay in the store.
  • Putting things right: an entry's Belongs to moves it to another car or to the store. A vehicle that was only a place to keep supplies: its Details have This isn't a car: move its entries to the store.
  • From Airtable: a cars base (tables Cars, Maintenance, Expenses, Repairs) comes in the same way as the zakat one (Settings › Bring in from Airtable, all four CSVs at once). Each car becomes a vehicle with its details and price, for the person you pick; maintenance, expenses and repairs go in its log as Service, Added and Repair, with their photos; and each car's plan takes the names you used and the gaps it saw (say, engine oil every 5,000 km or 6 months).
  • Saving an entry, a job or a reading closes it and takes you back to where you were (the car, or the Vehicles page), with a message saying what was saved. Pick the photos or the bill in Add entry itself. The car's own button says Save car details: it's only for the car.
  • Ticked but not logged: a job ticked in Microsoft To Do, or whose + Upkeep was closed, is remembered. The car, the Vehicles overview and My Day show "Engine oil, ticked on …: add what it cost?". Log it opens Add entry for the day it was done; Skip stops asking.
  • Doing a job: tick its task and + Upkeep opens with the job filled in, dated the day you said it was done; or add the entry yourself. Either way the next round counts from it.

Zakat

Zakat is in the sidebar (on a phone, under More, with the Vault and Data). It works from each person's zakat day, set in Settings › People, for example 15th Sha'ban.

  • Islamic dates are worked out from the Umm al-Qura calendar. Today's is under Butler in the sidebar, and in My Day's line on a phone. If the month began on a different day where you are, click that date (or the Islamic date beside a date in Add rates or Make record) and pick how it is here: up to two days later or earlier. It corrects that whole month everywhere in Butler, on both devices, so you do it once a month, whenever you notice it drifting.
  • Records are named by both dates, for example 15th Sha'ban 1447 (Tue 3 Feb 2026). Dates on the Zakat, Assets and Vault pages always carry the year; on tasks and the calendar only when it isn't this year.
  • Colours: a year past its Due by with money left is red and says overdue; one still to pay is amber (left); paid or overpaid is green. The bars, the Left to pay card and the Pay zakat task ("overdue" in its title) follow the same.
  • The zakat day starts when someone becomes sahib-e-nisab: their zakatable wealth goes above the nisab and stays above it for a whole Islamic year (a dip within that year starts the count again). Butler works this out from the balances and rates you've entered and suggests it on the Zakat page; press Set as zakat day. It's only as exact as your entries, so keep balances and rates up to date, above all on the zakat day.
  • After that only the zakat day counts. Below the nisab on it, nothing is due and that zakat day ends; the next time the wealth goes above the nisab, a new count starts.
  • On the zakat day you get an "Update balances and rates" task, so the record has that day's figures.
  • + Zakat record: on or after the zakat day, press + Zakat record (a reminder task comes on the day). It lists every zakat day since the zakat day began that has no record, oldest first (each also shows on the Overview). If you don't want a record for one, press No record on its line on the Overview: it goes, and Records lists it under "No record kept for" with Bring back. It warns about any rates or balance that's out of date for that day. It lists what the person had that day, their share of each asset, valued at that day's rates. Property, vehicles and other personal things start unticked; tick or untick anything. You can change the rates, or type a value to replace the worked-out one. It saves as you go.
  • The sum: the nisab is 52.5 tola of silver at that day's silver rate. Above it, zakat is 2.5% of the total, rounded up to the next 1,000 unless you untick that. Mark done freezes the record: later changes to assets or rates don't touch it. Reopen makes it a draft again.
  • Paying: it's due by the next zakat day; change Due by in the record if it's different (clear it to go back). + Payment (with who received it, if you like) counts towards the oldest year with money left, or the year you pick. As you type the amount it shows what will be left; going over shows straight away whether the extra counts towards next year, is kept as extra charity, or is split across years with money left.
  • Tasks: each person gets "Make zakat record" on their zakat day and "Pay zakat" a week before the next one, ticked by themselves when the record is made or paid. Someone else's carry their name and don't go to your To Do. Delete one and it isn't made again.
  • Overview: the next zakat day, what's left to pay, the latest year, whether the person is above the nisab today (from what usually counts), and each year's payments against what was due.
  • Rates are the same as on the Assets page.

Utilities

The Utilities page (sidebar; on a phone, under More) keeps your electricity, gas, water, phone and internet: an Overview tab and one tab per type. Any other type you type in (say, Security) gets its own tab.

  • + Connection: its name (House, Shop), provider, account or consumer number, the property it's for (from Assets, optional) and whose it is. Give the day of the month the bill is due and it gets a bill task: monthly on that day, reminding at 9 am (so it reaches your phone), in a Utilities bucket. Without a day, Make its bill task on its card does it later. Change the repeat in the task as usual.
  • The bill day is any day of the month or the last day of the month (30, 31 or 28/29, whichever that month has). If a month's bill comes due in the next month (October's bill due on 5 November), set The bill due then is for to the month before: the task says the month it's for ("Electricity bill (Oct 26)", due 5 Nov), ticking it records the bill for October, and the costs count it in October. Changing the day or the month later moves the next bill task.
  • Paid or not: Add a bill starts with Paid on empty; leave it so for a bill not paid yet, and it shows as Unpaid in the table and on the Overview. Ticking the bill task fills in the day you ticked it. As you type, the pop-up shows the rate, the effective bill and the effective rate. A new bill stays open after saving, so you can see its file or add one.
  • The bill itself: a bill keeps its scan or photo: pick it as you save a new bill, or open a bill and Add file (on the phone also Take photo). It goes in the connection's Vault folder, named for the bill ("Electricity bill Oct 2026"), and shows on the bill with a preview and a paperclip in the table. A file attached to the bill task (or shared to it with Add to… before the bill comes) is offered when you tick it: under Files from the task each is ticked and goes on the bill, named like its other files; untick one to leave it only on the task.
  • Its bills go in the Vault: a file you attach to the bill task is saved in Utilities › Gas in your folder and shows there too. Once one person has two of a type, each gets its own folder (Utilities › Gas › House), and the bills already there move with it.
  • A connection opens as a sheet: what it cost this year and last, the last bill, the units and the rate they worked out at, and its bills as a table: month, units, bill, net metering credit, rate, effective bill (the bill less the credit) and effective rate. Costs count the effective bill; the table has a totals row, and the sheet and the Overview say what net metering saved this year.
  • Paste history (electricity) brings in a bill sheet you kept in Excel: select its rows (Month, Units, Bill, Net Meter Benefit; the worked-out columns after them can come too), copy and paste. It shows the months, units, bills, credit and effective total first, to check against your sheet; pasting again replaces those months.
  • Names: the bill task is the connection's name and the month the bill is for, "Electricity Plaza (Oct 26)". Files on a bill task, a rent task or in a bill's pop-up are named the same way, "Electricity Plaza Oct 2026.pdf", "Plaza lease Rent Nov 2026.jpg".
  • Ticking the bill task asks what it came to, with its units (litres for fuel; none for phone and internet) and any credit. Leave it empty to skip. Add a bill on its card adds one by hand (older bills, say); tap a bill to change or delete it.
  • Overview: pick a Property (or all, or those not linked to one) and a Year (or All time), beside Filter this page; the choice stays as you move between tabs. Filter this page finds a connection by its name, property, provider or account number. At the top: this month (with what's unpaid), the year so far against the same months last year, and the average a month. Then a table per type: the column heads at the top, a shaded first row with the type's totals, then a numbered row per connection. The columns: its last bill (Paid or Unpaid), the type's own figures (units, rate and net metering for electricity; units and rate for gas and water; litres and price a litre for fuel), the year so far, the change against last year, a 12-month trend line and when the next bill is due. On a phone the table scrolls sideways inside its card. At the bottom, month by month against last year. Each type's tab shows its group above its connections' cards.
  • Fuel: the pump that bills you monthly is a Fuel connection; tick the cars it fuels (sold ones aren't offered). Each month's bill has its amount and litres, and can be split by car (amount, litres and the reading), as the pump's statement shows it; left empty, the bill and litres are the cars' added up. Each car's sheet then shows its fuel: this year's cost and litres, km a litre and fuel a km (from the readings), and what it cost with its upkeep. The readings count as the car's readings too. If it also fuels something that isn't a car, type it in Also fuels (Generator; several with commas): each bill then has a row for it (amount and litres), and the connection's card on the Fuel tab shows This year by car and by each of them, with Not split for any part of a bill typed as one figure.
  • Upkeep on the fuel bill: when the pump's statement also charges for an oil change, a filter or a wash, the bill's Upkeep on this bill lists the cars' upkeep from that month and the one before that has no cost yet: things logged without a cost, and jobs you ticked but didn't log. Type what each cost. One more (a wash, say) can be typed under them. Each goes in the car's upkeep log with its cost (a ticked job is logged on the day you ticked it), and the log says on the fuel bill, with a link to the bill and its scan. The car rows stay fuel only, so the price a litre leaves upkeep out; left empty, the bill adds it in, and This year shows it as Upkeep. Emptying a cost later takes it off the bill.
  • Does it add up? Under a fuel bill's parts, a line adds up the cars, what else it fuels and the upkeep as you type, and says in red how much isn't split or is more than the bill (litres too). If you've typed the bill and its parts come to something else, Save asks first: Fix it takes you back with everything as you typed it; Save anyway saves. Left empty, the bill is the parts added up, with no question.
  • A property lists its utilities and what they cost this year in its pop-up.
  • Deleting a connection sends it, its bills and its open bill task to the Bin.

Vault

The Vault (sidebar; on a phone, under More) keeps documents: IDs, passports, licences, deeds, certificates, insurance.

  • + Document: its kind (IDs, Travel, Property papers, Certificates, Other), type, name, number, issue and expiry dates, and notes. Lists show only the last four characters of a number; open it to see all of it.
  • Scans: once it's saved, Add file (on the phone also Take photo) keeps a scan with it, in the same attachments folder as task files.
  • Renewing: a document that expires gets a "Renew …" task, 3 months before by default (or 1, 2, 6 or 12; or no reminder), with a reminder at 9 am that reaches your phone. Ticking it opens the document so you can type the new expiry date, and the next reminder follows from that. The Overview lists what needs renewing and what expires within a year.
  • Assets: a document can belong to assets, for example insurance to a car or a deed to a plot. The asset shows its documents (and can link another or start a new one), and the renew task names it. Transferring the asset offers to give its documents to the new owner; deleting the asset keeps its documents.
  • Assets have photos and files too, in the same way.
  • Whose: a document belongs to one or more people, and the person picker filters them. A family member's renew task is theirs, not your To Do.

Folders

The Folders tab shows the attachments folder as it is on disk. Each person's folder holds your own folders (Documents for documents with no folder of their own, Utilities, Rentals and any you make, like Me › Car papers › Insurance) and Butler's: Assets, Tasks, Upkeep and Musings (Me › Assets › Gold).

  • Outside the Vault the folders follow the things in them (a car, a repeating task). Tap a file to open it; the arrow opens what it's on. Add files in a thing's own folder (a car's) attaches them to it; in a folder of many things (Gold, Tasks) it asks which one, or Just keep it here as a plain file in that folder. New folders can't go inside Butler's folders, and a folder of yours can't take their names.
  • New folder makes one where you are, right in a person's folder or inside one of yours; folders can sit inside folders. The path at the top takes you back up. ✎ renames a folder (everything inside follows, its files on disk too) and the bin deletes it: if anything is inside, it asks first, and those things go to the Bin for 30 days.
  • Add files (on the phone also Take photo) puts plain files in the folder: bills, agreements, anything without dates. Each keeps its own name. Tap one to open it; ✎ shows its details, where you can rename it, move it to another folder or give it to someone else.
  • Removing a file: ✕ beside a file, wherever it's listed, asks first. A file on a task, asset or entry comes off it (and is deleted only when nothing else uses it); a plain file (a bill's scan, an agreement, a file in your folders) goes to the Bin for 30 days.
  • A plain file can become a document: in its details, tick Keep its dates and number and fill them in; anything that expires then gets its renew reminder.
  • + Document in a folder puts the new document there. Any document's details have a Folder to move it.
  • From Vault: a task, an asset or an upkeep entry can use a file that's already in the Vault: pick it under From Vault… beside its files. It's the same file, not a copy (it shows a small ID mark), so renaming it anywhere renames it everywhere, and removing it from the task leaves it in the Vault.
  • A task's files straight into the Vault: in a task's panel, Files go to picks a Vault folder instead of Tasks (for example Vault › Bills › Electricity). Every file it gets from then on is saved there and shows there as a plain file, while staying on the task. For a repeating task it covers every copy, so each month's bill lands in the same folder. Choosing or changing the folder offers to move the files already on it; going back to Tasks leaves the ones in the Vault where they are. Finishing or deleting the task leaves its bills in the Vault.

Bringing in an Airtable zakat base

If you kept zakat in an Airtable base with the tables Gold, Silver, Overview, Zakat Summary and Zakat Payments, Butler can bring it all in:

  2. In Butler, Settings › Bring in from Airtable › Choose CSV files, and pick all five at once. Each is recognised by its columns, whatever its name.
  3. Say who each name in the base is: you, someone already in People, a new person, or left out.
  4. Gold and silver that weren't linked to anyone there weren't counted, so they're left out unless you say whose they are.
  5. Bring in. Rates, savings, gold and silver become assets; each person gets their zakat day and since when they've been sahib-e-nisab; every year becomes a done record with what was due (and its due date); every payment goes to its year.

Chapter 3

Repeating tasks

A task that comes back makes its next copy when you tick it, carrying its name, files and reminders forward.

How repeats work

A repeating task has one open copy at a time. Tick it off and its next copy appears, due on the next date, with the same steps (unticked), reminders, labels and 📌 files.

Rent (Nov) · due 1 Nov✓ tick →Rent (Dec) · due 1 Dec
  • Kinds: daily, weekdays, weekly on chosen days, monthly (by date, or "the last Friday"), yearly, every N of any of these, and again after I finish it, counted from the day you tick it.
  • Ends: never, on a date, or after a number of times.
  • A repeating task always keeps a due date: its next copies are counted from it.
  • If you miss some, the missed copies stay as overdue. To catch up instead, use "again after I finish it".

The moving part

The part in brackets moves forward with each copy, while the title stays the same: Rent (Nov 26), then Rent (Dec 26). Starting a repeat on "Pay rent Nov" turns "Nov" into the moving part for you, in the panel and in quick add.

Renaming a repeating task renames every copy, done ones too; each keeps its own moving part.

Unticking and changed copies

Unticking takes the next copy away again, unless you've changed it since it was made (notes, steps, its date…). Then Butler asks "Remove it anyway?" and says what you changed.

For "again after I finish it" repeats, changing the done date moves the next copy too. If you've changed that copy, it asks "Move it anyway?": Yes moves only its date and keeps your changes.

Files on repeats

A file attached to a repeating task stays with that copy only, like this month's bill. Click 📌 on a file to keep it on every copy instead, like an account-details sheet. A copy's History shows the earlier copies with their files.

Chapter 7

How figures are worked out

Every number Butler shows can be checked by hand: here is each sum in words, with a worked example.

What an asset is worth

Gold and silver: the weight in tola (grams ÷ 11.7) × that day's rate for its karat (rupees per tola). Cash, accounts, investments: the latest amount on or before the day, × the currency's rate if it isn't rupees. Debts count against you. An asset with no rate for the day says can't be valued rather than counting as nothing.

Your part of something shared is its value × your share on that day.

ExampleSumResult
A 46.8 g, 22 karat set; 22 karat at Rs 2,50,000 a tola46.8 ÷ 11.7 = 4 tola; 4 × 2,50,000Rs 10,00,000
Half of it is yours10,00,000 × 50%Rs 5,00,000

Zakat

On the zakat day: add up your part of everything ticked as counting. The nisab is 52.5 tola of silver at that day's silver rate. If the total is above it, zakat is 2.5% of the total; with Round up on, that goes up to the next 1,000. Payments count towards the oldest year with something left; paying more than is left carries over or stays as extra charity, as you choose.

ExampleSumResult
Silver at Rs 2,600 a tola52.5 × 2,600Nisab Rs 1,36,500
Counted Rs 12,34,000 (above the nisab)12,34,000 × 2.5%Rs 30,850
Round up onup to the next 1,000Rs 31,000 due

Rent owed

Each month from the agreement's start to this month (or the month it ended) owes that month's rent from its term. Owed is the rent of every month less what was received and what was written off. Renewing suggests 10% more than the rent now, unrounded.

Bills and utilities

The effective bill is the bill less any credit (net metering, a refund). The rate is the bill ÷ the units; the effective rate is the effective bill ÷ the units. Rates show in whole rupees, as a bill sheet does. Totals on the Utilities page count effective bills. vs last year is (this period − the same months last year) ÷ the same months last year.

A figure looks wrong

  1. Check the day: values and zakat use the rates on or before that day, so an old rate may be in use. Add the day's rates.
  2. Check whose: with one person picked at the top, only their part shows.
  3. Open the Data page and find the rows behind it (Filter this page helps); a row opens its record.
  4. Do the sum by hand with this chapter. If it still differs, note which row and which figure.

When something's wrong

Look for the words you see on screen in the first column: it says why, and what to do.

Calendar colours

Overdue Today Coming up Done Event Done late Coming repeat

Only three things have a colour: late (overdue, or missed and done later) is red, today is teal, and everything else is plain ink: coming up on a light fill, done faded and struck through, events outlined, repeats dashed.

Coming repeats are previews, not tasks yet; "again after I finish it" ones are estimates (≈). Click a task on the calendar to open it. A label on the calendar shows the name first; point at it (or open the day) for its time.

On the phone each day shows how many things it holds instead of names, in red when something there is late. Tap a day and it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen; tap outside it or × to close.

In a day's list, a done task is simply crossed out, and a late one has a small line under its name: how late it was, and the other day.
